# Gendered grade gaps at five universities

Across five Big Ten universities, large STEM lecture courses graded women lower than men with similar academic
records, while lab courses did not. The pattern held across campuses, disciplines and terms, which points to how
courses are structured and graded rather than to students.

## Question

Once prior performance is accounted for, do women and men earn different grades in large introductory courses at
several universities, and how consistent is the pattern across disciplines and course formats?

## Data

- Five public research universities in the Big Ten Academic Alliance, together enrolling more than 150,000
  undergraduates a year
- 1,122,586 enrollments in 249 introductory courses across 13 disciplines, six academic years
- Each campus kept its own records and ran the same shared R code on them

## Approach

- Grade anomaly: the course grade minus the student's GPA in other courses; a course's average is its grade
  penalty or bonus
- Gendered performance difference (GPD): women's average grade anomaly minus men's; negative favors men
- Propensity-score optimal matching term by term, with covariates chosen partly by LASSO; matching reported
  because it gave the most conservative estimates

## Findings

- In 172 STEM courses (677,949 enrollments), 74% of lecture courses and 93% of mixed courses gave grade
  penalties, while 64% of lab courses gave grade bonuses [Results, Figure 1]
- Average GPDs in STEM: −.07 for lectures, −.10 for mixed courses, .01 for labs [Results, Figure 1]
- The lecture-versus-lab contrast appeared in biology, chemistry, engineering and physics; large general
  chemistry showed especially large penalties and gaps [Results, Figure 2]
- Outside STEM, accounting and economics lectures averaged a GPD of −.14, writing courses .06 [Results, Figure 3]
- In selected courses, gaps held steady across terms even as instructors and students changed [Results, Figure 4]

## Why it matters

Consistent gaps across campuses suggest equity work should examine how large lectures are structured and graded,
such as timed high-stakes exams. Small GPA differences still matter for scholarships, honors and jobs.
