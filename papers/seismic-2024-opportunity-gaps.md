# Opportunity gaps across 3,207 course sections

About a third of introductory STEM sections taken by would-be biology majors showed small or no grade gaps for
disadvantaged students. That suggests equitable outcomes are achievable, and that course design, not students,
drives the gaps.

## Question

How much do grade gaps for biology-intending students vary from one course section to another, and how do they
relate to discipline, institution and course features?

## Data

- Five public research universities in the SEISMIC collaboration
- First-year, full-time, non-transfer students intending to major in biology; fall 2013 to 2019 cohorts, each
  followed for two years
- 292,591 enrollments in 3,207 sections of biology, chemistry, mathematics and physics

## Approach

- Gaps for four comparisons: women vs. men; PEER vs. non-PEER students; first-generation vs.
  continuing-generation; low-income vs. not
- Three gap measures, including a quantile-regression rank score adjusted for prior performance
- Hierarchical clustering of sections by their gap profiles; ANOVA and chi-square tests by discipline and
  institution

## Findings

- Adjusted gaps were negative for every comparison: largest for PEER students (mean −0.37), then first-generation
  (−0.35 to −0.32), low-income (−0.27 to −0.24) and women (−0.19 to −0.16) [Table 4]
- Sections fell into three clusters: large gaps (1,720 sections, 54%), mixed (352, 11%) and small gaps (1,135,
  35%) [Tables 7–8]
- Biology sections were overrepresented among small-gap sections (40%); chemistry, mathematics and physics were
  underrepresented [Results, RQ3]
- Gaps differed across institutions for all four comparisons, and no campus had the smallest gaps throughout
  [Table 6]

## Why it matters

The authors attribute gaps mainly to course structures and teaching practice. They recommend giving faculty
section-level equity analyses and studying the small-gap sections as models.
