# How fast galaxies move in maxBCG clusters

Using galaxy velocities around the central galaxies of maxBCG clusters, the paper measures both the typical
velocity spread at a given cluster richness and how much it varies, a step toward tying richness to mass.

## Question

At fixed optical richness, what are the mean and the scatter of cluster velocity dispersion?

## Data

- maxBCG clusters from about 7500 square degrees of SDSS imaging [Sec. 2.1]
- Catalog extended to 0.05 < z < 0.31 and richness of 3 or more: 195,414 clusters and groups [Sec. 3.2]
- With spectroscopic coverage: 12,253 clusters and 57,298 galaxy redshifts [Sec. 3.2]
- Tests on the C4 spectroscopic cluster catalog and on N-body mock catalogs [Sec. 2.2, 5.3]

## Approach

- Stack clusters by richness and measure velocity differences between each BCG and its neighbors [Sec. 4]
- Fit the stacked histograms with two Gaussians plus a background [Sec. 4.2]
- Model the non-Gaussian shape as a lognormal mix of dispersions to recover the scatter [Sec. 5.1]
- Correct for BCG and fitting biases, checked against single-cluster dispersions and mocks [Sec. 5.2.1]

## Findings

- Mean dispersion rises from 202 ± 10 km/s in small groups to more than 854 ± 102 km/s in rich clusters [Abstract, Table 2]
- Scatter is at most 40.5 ± 3.5%, falling to 14.9 ± 9.4% in the richest bin [Abstract, Table 2]
- On mocks the scatter is recovered within 1 sigma and the mean within 10% [Abstract, Sec. 5.3.2]
- A lognormal scatter model fits better than a Gaussian one [Sec. 5.3.4]
- Little dependence on local density; more luminous BCGs sit in clusters with higher dispersions [Sec. 6.2, 6.3]

## Why it matters

Velocity dispersion relates to mass in a well-understood, low-scatter way, so the authors present these
measurements as a bridge between richness and theory, limited mainly by velocity bias.
