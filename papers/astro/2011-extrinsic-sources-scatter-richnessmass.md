# What blurs cluster galaxy counts

A cluster's galaxy count is noisier than its mass alone would predict. This paper tests which observational
effects cause the extra noise, and finds that picking the wrong center galaxy matters far more than the rest.

## Question

Which observational, or "extrinsic," effects add enough scatter to the richness–mass relation to matter for
photometric cluster surveys such as DES and LSST?

## Data

- Monte Carlo simulations of cluster and field galaxies, with at least 400 and up to 10,000 realizations per
  setup [Sec. 1, 3]
- SDSS and maxBCG data to calibrate backgrounds, photometric errors and density boosts near the 2000 richest
  maxBCG clusters [Sec. 2.4, 4.4, 5.1]
- Millennium Gas Simulation, used to illustrate projected structure [Sec. 5.2; Fig. 8]

## Approach

- Measured richness with a matched filter (λ) that weights galaxies by position, brightness and color [Sec. 2]
- Added one effect at a time: halo triaxiality, red-sequence color offsets, photometric and photo-z errors,
  miscentering and projection [Sec. 4–5]
- Counted an effect as relevant if it raised the scatter by more than 0.05 above Poisson [Sec. 3; App. A]

## Findings

- Miscentering is by far the biggest source of extra scatter, especially for low-richness clusters [Sec. 4.6;
  Sec. 6]
- With Poisson scatter plus miscentering, total scatter at fixed mass is about 0.48 at λ = 30 and 0.23 at
  λ = 60 [Sec. 4.6]
- Triaxiality is borderline, adding about 0.04. Color offsets and photometric errors are negligible [Sec. 4.2–4.4]
- Photo-z errors are negligible below Δz ≈ 0.02; maxBCG's is about 0.008 [Sec. 4.5; Fig. 3]
- Severe projection hits about 1–5% of halos but about 5–15% of optically selected clusters [Abstract; Sec. 5.2]

## Why it matters

For a DES-like survey, the scatter must be known to about 0.05 or dark-energy parameters come out biased
[App. A]. The paper identifies which effects need careful survey-specific modeling.
