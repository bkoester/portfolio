# Weighing SDSS clusters with weak lensing

By stacking the faint distortions that maxBCG clusters imprint on background galaxies, the paper measures
average mass profiles for about 130,000 clusters across a wide span of scales.

## Question

What do the stacked weak-lensing profiles of maxBCG clusters look like, and how do they depend on cluster
richness, luminosity and redshift?

## Data

- SDSS DR4 imaging [Sec. 3.1, Fig. 1]
- 27,912,891 source galaxies brighter than r = 22 [Sec. 3.2]
- 132,473 maxBCG clusters with photometric redshift 0.1 to 0.3 [Sec. 3.5]
- 12 richness bins and 16 luminosity bins [Fig. 8, Fig. 11]

## Approach

- Convert background-galaxy shear to excess surface density around clusters [Sec. 2]
- Neural-network photometric redshifts for sources, trained on about 60,000 spectra [Sec. 3.4]
- Subtract residual signal around random points and correct for cluster members diluting the sources [Sec. 4]
- Jackknife error estimates [Sec. 5]

## Findings

- Profiles detected from 25 kpc/h to 30 Mpc/h, at significance of 15-20 per bin [Abstract, Fig. 8]
- Signals calibrated to about 10%; source redshift distribution is the main remaining uncertainty [Abstract, Sec. 6.1]
- At fixed richness, more luminous clusters show stronger signal, hinting luminosity tracks mass better [Sec. 5, Figs. 10-11]
- No detectable redshift evolution over 0.1 < z < 0.3 [Sec. 5, Fig. 12]
- Simple power laws do not describe the profiles well [Sec. 5, Table 1, Fig. 9]

## Why it matters

These measurements are the input for companion papers that turn them into mass-observable relations, mass-to-light
ratios and cosmological constraints.
