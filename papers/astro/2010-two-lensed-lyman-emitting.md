# Two magnified galaxies from the early universe

Galaxy clusters found in the Sloan Giant Arcs Survey magnify two very distant galaxies at z ~ 5 enough to
study each one individually rather than only in stacks.

## Question

What are the luminosities, star formation rates and stellar masses of two strongly lensed Lyman-alpha emitting
galaxies at z ~ 5?

## Data

- Found as r-band dropouts in Gemini North/GMOS griz imaging of Sloan Giant Arcs Survey clusters [Sec. II.1]
- GMOS nod-and-shuffle spectroscopy and APO/NIC-FPS near-infrared imaging [Sec. II.1]
- Lensing clusters SDSS J0915+3826 (z = 0.397) and SDSS J1343+4155 (z = 0.418) [Sec. II.2-II.3]

## Approach

- Confirmed redshifts from the Lyman-alpha emission line [Sec. II]
- Built lens models with LENSTOOL to correct for magnification [Sec. III.1]
- Fit stellar population models to the photometry [Sec. III.2]

## Findings

- SGAS J091541+382655 lies at z = 5.200 and SGAS J134331+415455 at z = 4.994 [Abstract; Sec. II]
- Magnifications of about 10 or more and about 12 [Sec. III.1]
- Rest-frame Lyman-alpha equivalent widths of 25.3 ± 4.1 Å and 135.6 ± 20.3 Å [Abstract]
- Corrected Lyman-alpha star formation rates of at most 5.3 ± 1.0 and 4.1 ± 0.6 solar masses per year [Sec. III.1]
- Among the brightest known at z ~ 5 as seen, but intrinsically fainter once lensing is removed [Sec. IV]

## Why it matters

The authors present them as the first z ~ 5 galaxies suited to detailed follow-up and recommend work with
8-10 m telescopes, HST and JWST.
