# Distant supernovae in old, dust-poor galaxies

Type Ia supernovae in old elliptical galaxies at very high redshift behave differently from those in
star-forming galaxies and suffer little dust, which makes them cleaner distance markers for cosmology.

## Question

At z > 0.9, do Type Ia supernova properties depend on their host galaxies, and can early-type hosts single out
supernovae that are well-behaved standard candles?

## Data

- HST ACS F775W and F850LP imaging of 25 clusters at 0.9 < z < 1.46, 4 to 9 visits each [Sec. II]
- Supernovae from the HST Cluster SN Survey plus the GOODS fields [Sec. III]
- Host spectroscopy from Keck/DEIMOS, Subaru/FOCAS and VLT/FORS [Sec. III.3]

## Approach

- Built a composite red sequence from spectroscopically confirmed cluster members [Sec. IV.2]
- Classified hosts as early- or late-type from color, Gini/asymmetry morphology and [O II] emission [Sec. IV.1; Sec. V]
- Used supernova rates in passive hosts to check typing without spectra [Sec. VI]
- Used the red-sequence scatter to limit dust in early-type hosts [Abstract]

## Findings

- 6 of 9 cluster hosts and 4 of 9 field hosts in the HST survey are early-type, plus 7 of 24 GOODS hosts [Sec. V]
- Supernovae in early-type hosts rise and fade faster than those in late-type hosts, a first confirmation at z > 0.9 [Abstract]
- Both host groups give supernovae with similar color distributions [Abstract]
- Reddening in early-type cluster hosts is likely E(B−V) ≲ 0.06 [Abstract]
- A tentative host-mass versus Hubble-residual trend appears, with a small sample and large errors [Abstract]

## Why it matters

The authors argue early-type hosts are a favorable setting for high-redshift standard candles, and that
surveys targeting massive galaxies may pick up a brightness bias that cosmology analyses must account for.
