# A quasar split six ways by a cluster

A galaxy cluster bends the light of a distant quasar into six images, the first cluster-lensed quasar known
with that many. Its predicted time delays make it useful for future measurements.

## Question

What is SDSS J2222+2745, and what does a lens model predict for its images and time delays?

## Data

- Candidate found in the Sloan Giant Arcs Survey using SDSS DR8 [Abstract, Sec. 2]
- Nordic Optical Telescope MOSCA imaging, September 2011 and September 2012 [Sec. 2, Table 1]
- NOT ALFOSC spectroscopy, August and September 2012 [Sec. 3.1-3.2]
- Quasar at z = 2.82, cluster at z = 0.49, giant arc at z = 2.30 [Abstract]

## Approach

- Find fainter images by g-i difference imaging after PSF matching [Sec. 3.2]
- Model the cluster core with GALFIT for image photometry [Sec. 3.3]
- Build Lenstool lens models with five and with six images [Sec. 4, Table 5]

## Findings

- Five images confirmed by spectra, a sixth supported by photometry and spectroscopy [Abstract]
- Maximum image separation of 15.1 arcseconds [Abstract, Sec. 3.4]
- Some images vary by roughly 10-30% between epochs [Abstract]
- Predicted time delays from about 100 days to about 6 years [Abstract, Table 6]
- Models fit image positions to 0.14 and 0.18 arcsec rms [Sec. 4]

## Why it matters

It is only the third known cluster-lensed quasar; the authors note its delays are measurable with 2-4 m
telescopes and that such systems can help constrain sigma_8 and Omega_M.
