# Optically found clusters have younger galaxies

Hubble images of distant clusters show that the red galaxies in optically selected clusters stopped forming
stars later than those in X-ray selected clusters, which hints that the optical clusters are less mature.

## Question

At z ~ 1, does the spread in color of early-type cluster galaxies differ between optically selected and X-ray
selected clusters, and what does that imply about when their stars formed?

## Data

- HST ACS imaging in F775W and F850LP from the HST Cluster SN Survey (program 10496) [Sec. II]
- 9 optically selected clusters (8 from RCS, 1 from SpARCS) at 0.9 < z < 1.23 [Abstract; Sec. II; Table 1]
- 4 X-ray selected comparison clusters at z of about 1.03-1.27 [Table 1]

## Approach

- Picked out elliptical and S0 galaxies automatically from their light profiles (Gini and asymmetry) [Sec. II]
- Fit the color-magnitude relation within 0.5 h^-1 Mpc and measured its scatter, with bootstrap errors [Sec. III]
- Compared the scatter with single-burst stellar population models to find when star formation ended [Sec. IV; Fig. 1]

## Findings

- Mean observed-frame color scatter is 0.049 ± 0.008 for the optical clusters and 0.031 ± 0.007 for the X-ray clusters [Abstract; Sec. IV]
- A t-test rejects at 95% that the two samples come from one population [Sec. IV]
- Best-fit star formation cut-off redshifts are about 1.6 (optical) and 2.1 (X-ray), roughly 0.9 Gyr apart [Abstract; Fig. 1]
- X-ray clusters have a higher early-type fraction in their cores; both reach the field value by 0.5 h^-1 Mpc [Sec. V; Fig. 2]
- Richness does not differ significantly between the two samples [Sec. V]

## Why it matters

The authors suggest optically selected clusters catch clusters at an earlier stage of formation, while noting
that X-ray data for the whole optical sample are needed to confirm the link.
