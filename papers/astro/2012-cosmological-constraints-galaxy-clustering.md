# Cosmology from cluster mass per galaxy

Combining how galaxies cluster with the ratio of cluster mass to member-galaxy count constrained the matter
density and the clumpiness of the universe. The method does not rely on cluster counts.

## Question

Can galaxy clustering plus the cluster mass-to-galaxy-number ratio (M/N) separate galaxy bias from cosmology,
and so constrain Ωm and σ8 independently of cluster abundance?

## Data

- SDSS DR7 galaxy clustering over 7300 deg², in three volume-limited samples (M_r < −19.5, −20.5, −21.0) of
  112,497, 117,588 and 77,381 galaxies [Sec. 2.1; Table 1]
- maxBCG clusters from 6325 deg² at 0.1 ≤ z ≤ 0.3 with N200 ≥ 9, in six richness bins [Sec. 2.2; Table 2]
- Weak-lensing cluster masses from Sheldon et al. (2009b) [Sec. 2.2]

## Approach

- Modeled how galaxies occupy dark-matter halos (a halo occupation distribution) to predict both clustering and
  M/N [Sec. 3]
- Folded in nuisance parameters for the mass function, halo bias, galaxy evolution and lensing calibration
  [Sec. 4]
- Explored 21 parameters with MCMC [Sec. 5.1]

## Findings

- Clustering plus M/N alone: Ωm^0.5 σ8 = 0.465 ± 0.026, Ωm = 0.29 ± 0.03, σ8 = 0.85 ± 0.06 [Abstract]
- Adding WMAP7: Ωm = 0.290 ± 0.016 and σ8 = 0.826 ± 0.020 [Abstract; Table 5; Fig. 8]
- Best fit χ² = 26.9 for 38 data points [Sec. 5.1]
- The constraints are insensitive to current uncertainties in the halo mass function and mass–richness relation
  [Sec. 6]

## Why it matters

The authors describe M/N as a method complementary to cluster abundances, with different systematics, for
constraining cosmology with future galaxy surveys.
