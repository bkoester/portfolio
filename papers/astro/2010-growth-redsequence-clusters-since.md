# Faint red cluster galaxies arrived late

Near-infrared images of distant clusters show fewer faint red galaxies, relative to bright ones, than in nearer clusters. That suggests faint cluster galaxies stopped forming stars later than bright ones.

## Question

How has the red sequence of early-type galaxies in rich clusters built up since z ~ 1?

## Data

- Deep Js- and Ks-band imaging of 15 clusters at z ~ 1 [main text].
- Clusters discovered in the Red-Sequence Cluster Survey (RCS-1) and observed with VLT/ISAAC [main text].

## Approach

- Build the combined Ks-band luminosity function and color-magnitude diagram at z = 1 [main text].
- Compute the ratio of luminous to faint red-sequence galaxies (L/F ratio) [main text].
- Compare with a simple model in which star formation stops earlier in bright galaxies than in faint ones [main text].

## Findings

- Ks-band luminosity function fit with Ks* = 18.82 and alpha = −0.42 [main text].
- L/F ratio at z = 1 is 1.07 ± 0.28 [main text].
- The L/F ratio rises by 100% from z = 0.45 to z = 1.0 [Abstract].
- The color-magnitude diagram shows a deficit of red-sequence galaxies fainter than MV = −20 [main text].
- The Ks* value favors passive evolution with formation redshift z_f = 3 [main text].

## Why it matters

The authors describe this as the first L/F ratio measured at z = 1 from a large cluster set, supporting later quenching of faint cluster galaxies.
