# Confirming fossil groups in X-rays

Picking fossil-group candidates from optical cluster data worked: most showed hot X-ray gas. They look like
ordinary groups in X-rays but have too few bright galaxies, which could bias galaxy-count cosmology.

## Question

Can fossil groups, dominated by one giant elliptical, be found efficiently from optical data and confirmed in X-rays?

## Data

- maxBCG clusters with 9 ≤ N200 ≤ 25 at 0.09 ≤ z ≤ 0.15 [Sec. II]
- 26 systems passed the optical cuts; 15 candidates remained after removing AGN, radio sources and bright stars
  [Sec. II; Table 1]
- Chandra ACIS-S snapshots of 5–10 ksec each [Sec. III.1]

## Approach

- Required a gap of more than 2.0 mag in i between the two brightest galaxies, and a luminous BCG [Sec. II]
- Fit X-ray spectra for temperature and surface brightness profiles for structure [Sec. III.2–III.3]
- Estimated hydrostatic masses and compared to scaling relations of normal groups and clusters [Sec. III.3; Sec. 4.2]

## Findings

- 12 of 15 candidates detected in X-rays, an 80% success rate; 87% counting one confirmed optically
  [Abstract; Sec. IV.1]
- Temperatures for 11 systems of 1.3–2.7 keV; masses M500 of 0.3–1.0×10^14 solar masses [Sec. V; Sec. III.3]
- 8 of 12 BCGs have a central X-ray source [Sec. III.4]
- Systems are optically underluminous by about a factor of three [Sec. IV.1]
- L_X–T_X shows no significant deviation from normal systems [Sec. 4.2; Fig. 6]

## Why it matters

Galaxy-poor systems may bias mass functions built from galaxy counts, and with them constraints on the power
spectrum normalization and Ωm.
