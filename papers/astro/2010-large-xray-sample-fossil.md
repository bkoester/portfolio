# X-ray checks on fossil galaxy groups

Fossil groups are systems dominated by one giant elliptical galaxy. Short Chandra observations of candidates
picked from the maxBCG catalog found hot X-ray gas in most of them, the first large X-ray sample of such systems
chosen optically.

## Question

Do optically selected fossil-group candidates show the extended X-ray emission expected of a group-scale halo,
and what are their X-ray properties?

## Data

- 15 fossil-group candidates selected from the maxBCG cluster catalog at 0.09 < z < 0.15 [Main text]
- Chandra snapshot observations of 14 of them, 5–10 ksec each [Main text]
- SDSS g, r, i imaging, shown for one example, SDSS J0856+0553 [Fig. 2]

## Approach

- Picked candidates with a bright central galaxy (L_BCG > 9 × 10^11 Lsun) and a gap of more than 2.0 magnitudes
  in i between it and the second-brightest galaxy within R200/2 [Main text]
- Drew from maxBCG clusters with richness 9 < N200 < 25 [Fig. 1]
- Searched the Chandra images for diffuse emission, and measured luminosity and temperature where the data
  allowed [Main text]

## Findings

- Diffuse X-ray emission detected from 11 of the 14 observed candidates at >90% confidence [Main text]
- X-ray luminosity and temperature measured for 9 of 14 [Abstract]
- These values span the range from large single ellipticals to rich groups [Abstract]

## Why it matters

Fossil groups were long thought to be old, undisturbed systems, but may be younger or more active. The authors
plan deep XMM follow-up of temperature, surface-brightness and abundance profiles to test how these common
systems formed.
