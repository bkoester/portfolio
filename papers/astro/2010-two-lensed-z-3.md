# Two magnified galaxies at redshift 3

Two galaxy clusters found in Sloan images act as natural magnifying glasses for two distant star-forming
galaxies, nearly doubling the number of such lensed galaxies known at the time.

## Question

What are two newly found strongly lensed Lyman Break Galaxies at z ~ 3 like, and how do their lens-corrected
brightnesses compare with ordinary, unlensed galaxies of this type?

## Data

- Discovered as u-band dropouts in SGAS follow-up imaging with the WIYN and Nordic Optical telescopes [Sec. II.1]
- Imaging from Gemini North/GMOS and CFHT/MegaCam (RCS-2) [Sec. II.2; Fig. 1]
- Magellan/MagE spectra: 6.1 hr for SGAS J122651.3+215220 and 1.5 hr for SGAS J152745.1+065219 [Sec. II.3]
- Comparison sample of 237 Lyman Break Galaxies from Steidel et al. (2003) [Sec. IV]

## Approach

- Measured redshifts from absorption lines in the spectra [Sec. II.3; Fig. 2]
- Measured arc brightness in apertures along each arc [Sec. II.2]
- Built a lenstool model for one cluster and explored a grid of simple models for the other [Sec. III; Fig. 3]

## Findings

- Source redshifts are z = 2.9233 and z = 2.7593, behind clusters at z = 0.43 and z = 0.39 [Table 1]
- Magnifications are about 40 for SGAS J122651 and at most about 15 for SGAS J152745 [Table 1; Sec. III]
- Lens-corrected r-band magnitudes are 24.61 and 23.52 [Table 1; Sec. IV]
- After correction, SGAS J122651 is fainter than 80% of the Steidel sample, while SGAS J152745 is among its brightest 25% [Sec. IV]

## Why it matters

The authors note the pair nearly doubles the known lensed Lyman Break Galaxies, giving bright targets for
detailed study, and that deeper imaging is needed to firm up the lens models.
