# Distant supernovae sharpen dark-energy limits

New Hubble measurements of distant Type Ia supernovae, many in galaxy clusters, were added to the Union
supernova compilation. They tighten dark-energy constraints beyond redshift 1, and the result still matches a
cosmological constant.

## Question

How much do new z > 1 supernovae from the HST Cluster Supernova Survey improve dark-energy constraints? Can the
survey build a sample hosted by dust-poor early-type galaxies?

## Data

- Photometry of 20 Type Ia supernovae at 0.623 < z < 1.415, from HST ACS and NICMOS plus Keck adaptive optics
  [Abstract; Sec. 3]
- 16 classified as secure or probable Type Ia [Sec. 2.1]
- Union2 compilation of 557 supernovae, updated here to Union2.1 with 580 [Sec. 1; Abstract]

## Approach

- Fitted light curves with SALT2, adding a term for host-galaxy mass [Sec. 4.1–4.2]
- Corrected NICMOS for count-rate non-linearity [Sec. 3.2.1]
- Split hosts into early and late types; treated systematics as nuisance parameters in a covariance matrix
  [Sec. 4.5]

## Findings

- Ten of the new supernovae are beyond z = 1 [Abstract]
- The dark-energy density constraint at 1.0 < z < 1.6 improves by 18%, systematics included [Abstract]
- Flat ΛCDM: ΩΛ = 0.729 ± 0.014 [Abstract]
- Constant equation of state: w = −1.013 (+0.068/−0.073) [Abstract]
- The survey raises the yield of early-type-hosted Type Ia supernovae by roughly a factor of four [Sec. 1]

## Why it matters

The authors say better limits on evolving dark energy need high-quality photometry of several dozen z > 1
supernovae. They suggest cluster fields observed with WFC3 on HST as an efficient way to find them.
