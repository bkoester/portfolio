# Spectra of clusters that bend light

Spectroscopy of 26 clusters that produce giant lensed arcs measured distances to the lensed background galaxies
and weighed the clusters by their galaxies' motions, finding them somewhat heavier than simulations predict.

## Question

What are the redshifts of the lensed sources and cluster members in strong-lensing clusters, and how massive are
these clusters compared with simulations?

## Data

- 26 strong-lensing clusters found by eye in SDSS and RCS-2, at 0.2 < z < 0.65; 17 known lenses and 9 new
  [Abstract; Sec. I]
- Gemini North GMOS multi-slit spectroscopy in 2008A and 2009A, plus APO 3.5 m spectra for two clusters
  [Sec. II; Table 1]
- 69 lensed background sources and 262 cluster members with redshifts [Abstract; Table 2]

## Approach

- Placed slits first on candidate arcs, then on red-sequence members; used nod-and-shuffle sky subtraction
  [Sec. II.3]
- Assigned redshifts with confidence classes [Sec. III.1]
- Velocity dispersions for clusters with ≥10 members, converted to mass with a simulation-calibrated
  relation [Sec. 3.2]
- Compared with a simulation prediction, correcting it for orientation bias [Sec. 4]

## Findings

- Lensed-source redshifts reach z = 5.200 [Abstract]
- Dynamical masses for 18 clusters with at least 10 members [Sec. 3.2; Table 3]
- Median mass 7.84×10^14 h_0.7^-1 solar masses, ~90% above the Hennawi et al. (2007) prediction of
  4.5×10^14 [Abstract; Sec. 4]
- Orientation bias estimated at 19–20%, raising the expected median to 5.36×10^14 [Sec. 4; Sec. 5]
- The excess is not significant given the uncertainties [Abstract]

## Why it matters

Lensing clusters probe dark-matter structure and magnify distant galaxies; this catalog supports both uses and
hints at stronger orientation bias.
