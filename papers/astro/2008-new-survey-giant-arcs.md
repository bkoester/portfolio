# Finding new giant gravitational arcs

Rich galaxy clusters picked out of SDSS images were re-imaged with deeper ground-based cameras to look for stretched giant arcs of background galaxies bent by gravity. The survey turned up dozens of new strong-lensing clusters, many bright enough for easy follow-up.

## Question

Can a cluster catalog built from SDSS photometry efficiently find new strong-lensing clusters with giant arcs at 0.1 ≲ z ≲ 0.6?

## Data

- Clusters found in SDSS DR5 photometry over about 8000 deg² with the Red Cluster Sequence algorithm [Sec. II; Abstract].
- Search volume of about 2 h^-1 Gpc³ [Sec. VI].
- 240 clusters imaged (195 with WIYN 3.5m, 45 with UH88), 141 with sub-arcsecond seeing [Abstract; Sec. VI].
- WIYN g-band (OPTIC and Minimo cameras) and UH88 V-band (UH8k camera) imaging [Sec. III].

## Approach

- Rank SDSS clusters by richness, detection significance and concentration for follow-up imaging [Sec. II].
- Two authors independently inspect each cluster's best-seeing image and grade candidate arcs 1 to 3 [Sec. IV].
- Combine the two grades into definite, likely and possible lens classes [Sec. IV].

## Findings

- 16 new definite giant-arc lenses (22 definite in all, 6 previously known) [Abstract; Table 1; Sec. VI].
- 12 new likely lenses (14 likely, 2 previously known) and 9 possible lenses [Abstract; Tables 2–3].
- The true number of new cluster lenses is estimated at about 30, against roughly 50 lensing clusters known before [Abstract; Sec. VI].
- 8 of the 11 previously known lenses that were imaged were recovered [Sec. VI].
- Many arcs are bright (g ≲ 21), easing spectroscopy [Sec. VI].

## Why it matters

The authors expect the new lenses to support systematic studies of lensing statistics for cosmology and structure formation, with a few possibly becoming showcase lenses like Abell 1689.
