# Are strong-lensing clusters too concentrated?

Ten clusters that bend background galaxies into arcs were weighed using their hot gas, seen through the Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) effect. Their arcs are about twice as large as their gas-based masses predict, hinting that these clusters pack mass more tightly at their centers than theory expects, or that the gas-based masses are biased.

## Question

Do strong-lensing clusters show higher central concentrations than predicted, when large-scale mass from the SZ effect is compared with small-scale Einstein radii?

## Data

- 10 clusters from the Sloan Giant Arc Survey and RCS2 Giant Arc program, plus RCS1 J2319 and Abell 1689 [Sec. II.1].
- All with spectroscopic arc redshifts; cluster redshifts 0.18 to 0.90 [Sec. II.1; Table 1].
- Sunyaev Zel'dovich Array observations at 31 GHz, 11 to 57 hours on source [Sec. II.2].

## Approach

- Fit a generalized pressure profile to the SZ data and convert Y500 to M500 with a weak-lensing-calibrated scaling relation [Sec. II.2].
- Measure Einstein radii from lens models, rescaled to fiducial lens and source redshifts [Sec. II.3].
- Compare with triaxial-halo predictions weighted for lensing selection, and run Monte Carlo tests [Sec. II.4; Sec. III.1].

## Findings

- Einstein radii are about twice as large as expected for the SZ-derived masses [Abstract; Sec. III.1; Fig. 1].
- A sample at least this discrepant appears in 2.8% of realizations (2.4% to 4.4% when the scaling normalization varies) [Sec. III.1].
- The discrepancy is only about 2 sigma [Sec. III.3].
- Lower gas fractions in lensing clusters could bias SZ masses low and explain the offset [Sec. III.2].

## Why it matters

The authors say the overconcentration problem "persists" in this sample, unless a systematic such as low gas fraction is at work, and plan to combine SZ, lensing and dynamical masses.
