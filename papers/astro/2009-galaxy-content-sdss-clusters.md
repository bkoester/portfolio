# What galaxies live in clusters

Using only SDSS images, the paper measures how many galaxies of each brightness and color sit in groups and clusters of different sizes. Satellite galaxy populations change little with cluster size in big clusters, the red-galaxy share varies with position, brightness, mass and time, and the central galaxy's brightness tracks cluster size closely.

## Question

How do satellite luminosity functions, red fractions and brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) luminosities depend on cluster richness, mass, radius and redshift?

## Data

- SDSS Data Release 4 imaging [Sec. II].
- 165,597 MaxBCG systems with N200 ≥ 3, 13,823 of them with N200 ≥ 10, at 0.1 ≤ z ≤ 0.3 [Sec. II.2].
- 7398.23 deg² after masking [Sec. III.3].
- Mass from a weak-lensing calibration of M200 against N200 [Eq. 10].

## Approach

- Count galaxies around clusters and subtract the background statistically using random points [Sec. III.1–III.2].
- Deproject to 3D profiles; estimate errors by jackknife over 12 sky regions [Sec. III.1].
- Fit Schechter luminosity functions to red and blue satellites [Sec. IV.1.1].
- Check the method on a mock catalog, recovering inputs to within about 1 sigma [Sec. III.6; Fig. 4].

## Findings

- Satellite luminosity function shape depends only weakly on richness in massive systems [Sec. IV.1.1; Fig. 7].
- Red fraction depends on radius, luminosity and mass, and rises about 5% between median redshifts 0.28 and 0.2 [Sec. IV.1.2; Fig. 11].
- BCG luminosity scales as M200^(0.30 ± 0.01) and N200^(0.38 ± 0.01) [Sec. IV.2; Eqs. 16–17].
- Scatter in BCG luminosity is sigma_logL ~ 0.17 for massive systems [Sec. IV.2; Fig. 13].
- BCGs give about 5% of the light in the most massive clusters and about 20% in 10^14 h^-1 M_sun systems [Sec. IV.2; Fig. 14].

## Why it matters

The authors show galaxy populations in clusters can be measured from photometry alone, a method suited to DES, LSST and Pan-STARRS.
