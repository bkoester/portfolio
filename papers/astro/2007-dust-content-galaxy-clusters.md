# Detecting dust inside galaxy clusters

Quasars seen behind galaxy clusters look slightly redder than quasars elsewhere, revealing a small amount of
dust spread through clusters, mostly in their outer parts.

## Question

Do galaxy clusters contain intracluster dust, how much, and where is it?

## Data

- About 10^4 optically selected maxBCG clusters at 0.1 < z < 0.3 from Koester et al. (2007) [Sec. II]
- SDSS DR5 spectroscopic quasars within 10 Mpc of cluster centers [Sec. II]
- About 3000 quasars in the innermost primary sample; quasars beyond 7 R200 as controls [Sec. II]
- Spectroscopic analysis with 1.5 < z < 2 quasars, about 27% of DR5 quasars [Sec. III]

## Approach

- Compared mean quasar colors in rings of distance from cluster centers, scaled by cluster radius, to
  redshift-matched control quasars [Sec. II]
- Built random control subsamples and null tests to measure noise and bias [Sec. II]
- Compared stacked spectra to separate dust reddening from lensing magnification [Sec. III]

## Findings

- Mean reddening of a few ×10^-3 mag for sight lines about 1 Mpc from cluster centers [Abstract; Fig. 1]
- The color changes across bands follow a standard dust reddening curve [Sec. III]
- Reddening falls roughly as 1/radius far out but is flat in the core, so most dust sits in the outskirts
  [Sec. III; Fig. 3]
- Implied dust-to-gas ratio is below 5% of the local interstellar value [Sec. IV]
- Magnification of background sources is "of order a few per cent" [Abstract; Sec. III]

## Why it matters

It settles a long-running question with a statistical detection, and suggests cluster centers hold little
interstellar-like dust.
