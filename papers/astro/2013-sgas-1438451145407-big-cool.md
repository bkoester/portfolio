# A magnified dusty starburst galaxy

A galaxy at redshift 0.816, magnified about 17 times by a foreground cluster, turns out to be a dusty, strongly star-forming system. Its dust is cooler than local galaxies of similar power, and its star formation spreads smoothly over a large disk.

## Question

What are the physical properties of a strongly lensed, infrared-luminous galaxy at z = 0.816, and how does it compare with local and distant star-forming galaxies?

## Data

- Lensing cluster SDSSJ 1438+1454 at z ≈ 0.237, found in SDSS and Spitzer IRAC imaging [Sec. II].
- Gemini GMOS and Magellan FourStar imaging, WISE 12 and 22 µm, Herschel PACS and SPIRE 70–500 µm [Sec. III].
- Keck NIRSPEC spectroscopy of H-alpha and [N II]; source z = 0.81589 ± 0.00005 [Sec. III.6; Sec. IV.3].

## Approach

- Build a LENSTOOL lens model fit with MCMC [Sec. IV.2].
- Fit stellar population and dust templates to the multiwavelength SED [Sec. IV.4].
- Measure size and shape in the image plane and in the reconstructed source plane [Sec. IV.8].

## Findings

- Total magnification 16.8 (+5.2/−6.2) [Sec. IV.2; Table 3].
- Total IR luminosity 4.0 (+1.8/−1.1) × 10^11 L_sun; stellar mass log(M*/M_sun) = 10.8 (+0.3/−0.2) [Sec. IV.4].
- Star formation rate 28 (+13/−7) M_sun/yr from H-alpha and 41 (+18/−11) M_sun/yr from far-IR [Sec. IV.6].
- Far-IR SED best matched by a local template with log L(TIR) = 10.75, far less luminous [Sec. IV.4; Fig. 10].
- Source-plane effective radius 4.3 ± 0.6 kpc; no evidence of an AGN [Sec. IV.8; Table 5; Sec. IV.5].

## Why it matters

The authors find it typical of z ~ 1 infrared galaxies in luminosity and size, with organized disk-like star formation that argues against a merger, and propose molecular gas follow-up.
