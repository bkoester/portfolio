# Supernova rates in distant galaxy clusters

Hubble watched 25 distant galaxy clusters for exploding white dwarfs. The rate of these explosions, far higher
than in nearby clusters, tests ideas about what kind of star system produces them.

## Question

How often do Type Ia supernovae explode in galaxy clusters at 0.9 < z < 1.46, and what does that say about
the delay between star formation and explosion?

## Data

- 25 clusters at redshifts 0.90 to 1.46, 24 with spectroscopic redshifts [Sec. II; Table 1]
- HST imaging every 20 to 26 days from July 2005 to December 2006 [Sec. II]
- 155 search visits; about 1000 candidates cut to 60 by automated and visual checks [Sec. III.1-III.2]

## Approach

- Sorted candidates into artifacts, active galactic nuclei and supernovae using light curves, hosts and
  spectra [Sec. III.3]
- Simulated detection efficiency with about 72,000 artificial point sources [Sec. IV.1]
- Measured cluster light and stellar mass to normalize the rate [Sec. V]
- Fit a power-law delay-time distribution assuming clusters formed in one burst at z = 3 [Abstract]

## Findings

- 8 ± 1 cluster Type Ia supernovae found [Abstract]
- Rate of 0.50 (+0.23/-0.19 stat, +0.10/-0.09 sys) h70² SNuB, about 5 ± 2 times the z < 0.2 cluster
  rate [Abstract]
- Delay-time power-law index of -1.41 (+0.47/-0.40) [Abstract]
- One host-less supernova limits the intracluster star fraction to below 0.47 at 95% confidence [Abstract]
- Shifting the assumed brightness by ±0.2 mag changes the rate by only 6-8% [Sec. IV.3]

## Why it matters

The authors find the result fits the double-degenerate (two white dwarf) picture and conflicts with some
single-degenerate models.
