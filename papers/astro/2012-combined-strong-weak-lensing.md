# Mass profiles of 28 lensing clusters

Combining strongly distorted arcs near cluster centers with subtle distortions farther out, this study measured
how concentrated the mass is in 28 clusters and found smaller clusters more concentrated than expected.

## Question

Are strong-lensing clusters more centrally concentrated than standard ΛCDM predicts, how does concentration
change with mass, and how elongated is the projected mass?

## Data

- 28 clusters from the Sloan Giant Arcs Survey, from z = 0.277 to z = 0.68 [Sec. 2.1; Table 1]
- Subaru/Suprime-cam g, r, i imaging taken 2007 June to 2011 April [Sec. 2.2]
- Arc and cluster redshifts largely from Gemini/GMOS [Sec. 2.1; Table 2]

## Approach

- Fit each cluster's Einstein radius from strong lensing together with its weak-lensing shear profile [Sec. 4.1]
- Modeled clusters as NFW halos described by mass and concentration [Sec. 4.1]
- Used ray-tracing through triaxial halos to predict the bias from selecting clusters by strong lensing [Sec. 4.3; App. A]
- Stacked shear from 25 clusters, and stacked 2D shear aligned to strong-lensing position angles [Sec. 5]

## Findings

- Concentration falls with mass as c_vir ∝ M_vir^(−0.59 ± 0.12), steeper than the predicted −0.2 [Abstract; Sec. 4.3]
- The stacked fit gives mean mass 4.57 (+0.33/−0.31) × 10^14 h^-1 solar masses and concentration 5.75 (+0.70/−0.57) [Sec. 5.1]
- Near 10^15 h^-1 solar masses concentrations roughly match lensing-biased expectations; near 10^14 they are much higher [Abstract]
- Mean projected ellipticity is 0.47 ± 0.06, a 5σ detection, close to the triaxial-halo prediction of 0.44 [Sec. 5.2]

## Why it matters

The authors suggest the excess concentration in lower-mass clusters may come from baryon cooling, and report that
luminous member galaxies trace the total mass well.
