# Un-stretching a magnified distant galaxy

Hubble images of a galaxy at redshift 1.7, magnified and smeared into a long arc by a foreground cluster, let the team build a precise lens model and reconstruct what the galaxy really looks like. The new model showed an earlier star-formation estimate was too high because it sampled an unusually magnified spot.

## Question

With HST imaging, how well can the lens be modeled and the galaxy RCSGA 032727-132609 be reconstructed, and how does that change earlier physical measurements?

## Data

- Lensed galaxy at z = 1.7 behind cluster RCS2 032727-132623, found in the RCS2 Giant Arc Survey [Abstract; Sec. 1].
- HST/WFC3 imaging on 2011 March 1 in seven filters (F390W to F160W), four orbits [Sec. 2].
- Giant arc about 38" long [Sec. 1].

## Approach

- Lens model in LENSTOOL with MCMC: 44 constraints, 19 free parameters [Sec. 3.2].
- Identify five images of the source and a secondary arc to constrain the model [Sec. 3.1].
- Ray-trace image pixels back through the deflection maps to the source plane [Sec. 3.3; Figs. 2–4].

## Findings

- Total source magnification 28.4 (+3.4/−2.7); giant arc average 25.1 (+3.2/−2.5) [Sec. 4].
- Magnification ranges from about 4 to over 100 across the arc [Sec. 4; Fig. 5].
- Adding the secondary arc cut magnification uncertainty from about 50% to about 10% [Sec. 3.2].
- The earlier spectroscopic slit region has magnification 42.2 ± 5.5, revising the star formation rate to 29 ± 8 M_sun/yr, now consistent with other estimates [Sec. 5].

## Why it matters

The authors say HST allows the first precise mapping of star-forming knots to the source plane, enabling knot-by-knot study of a z = 1.7 galaxy.
