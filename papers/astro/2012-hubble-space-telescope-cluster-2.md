# How often stars explode as Type Ia supernovae

Supernovae found behind and in front of distant clusters give the rate of Type Ia explosions out to z ≈ 1.6,
showing it stays high near z ~ 1 and levels off beyond.

## Question

How common are Type Ia supernovae per unit volume at high redshift, and how much do dust assumptions matter?

## Data

- HST ACS, 189 orbits, rolling search of 25 massive clusters at 0.9 < z < 1.46, July 2005 to December 2006
  [Abstract; Sec. II]
- 86 candidates; 60 passed light-curve cuts, classified as 29 supernovae, 17 AGN and 14 artifacts [Sec. II]
- About 20 of the 29 are Type Ia; 12 lie in cluster foregrounds and backgrounds [Abstract]

## Approach

- Divided Type Ia counts by effective time-volume searched, from simulated supernovae across each field [Sec. III]
- Assessed typing, host dust, luminosity, lensing and cosmic variance uncertainties [Sec. IV; Table 3]
- Recomputed rates under other studies' dust assumptions [Sec. 5.1–5.2; Table 4]

## Findings

- Rates of 1.18, 1.33 and 0.77 ×10^-4 h70^3 per yr per Mpc^3 at mean z of 0.807, 1.187 and 1.535 [Table 2]
- The rate is at least ~0.6×10^-4 at z ~ 1 and flattens at higher redshift [Abstract; Sec. 5.3]
- With one dust model, rates of 1.61 and 1.99 in the middle bins vs 1.30 and 1.32 from Dahlen et al. (2008)
  [Sec. 5.2; Table 4]
- Lensing gains and losses largely cancel; density effects are ≲5% [Sec. IV.2; Sec. IV.6]

## Why it matters

High-redshift rates constrain how long Type Ia progenitors take to explode; differing dust assumptions
can cause significant differences between studies.
