# How cluster X-ray brightness tracks mass

Stacking X-ray light and weak-lensing masses for thousands of optically found clusters shows that their X-ray
brightness rises with mass at the same rate seen in X-ray-selected samples.

## Question

What is the relation between mean X-ray luminosity and mean mass for optically selected clusters, and does it
agree with relations from X-ray-selected samples?

## Data

- 17,335 maxBCG clusters from SDSS, roughly volume-limited over 0.1 ≤ z ≤ 0.3 [Sec. 2.1]
- Six richness bins with N200 ≥ 9 [Table 1]
- ROSAT All-Sky Survey X-ray photons, 0.1-2.4 keV, about 400 s of exposure per cluster [Sec. 2.2]
- Mean masses from SDSS weak-lensing shear profiles [Sec. 2.3]

## Approach

- Stacked X-ray photons around each cluster's brightest galaxy in each richness bin [Sec. 2.2]
- Fit stacked lensing profiles with halo models, including a correction for mis-centering [Sec. 2.3]
- Combined the two power laws in richness into a luminosity-mass relation [Sec. 3]
- Modeled how correlated scatter in luminosity and richness at fixed mass would bias the result [Sec. 4]

## Findings

- Mean luminosity scales with mass to the power 1.65 ± 0.13, with a normalization of 12.6 (+1.4/−1.3) × 10^42 h^-2 erg/s at 10^14 h^-1 solar masses [Eq. 3]
- The slope agrees with X-ray-based estimates of 1.50 ± 0.08 and 1.59 ± 0.05 [Sec. 3]
- The normalization is about 30% (about 2σ) below the Reiprich & Böhringer value but agrees with Stanek et al. [Abstract; Sec. 3]
- The slope agreement suggests the covariance between X-ray luminosity and richness at fixed mass is not large [Sec. 4]

## Why it matters

The authors read the agreement as evidence that optically selected maxBCG clusters trace the same massive halo
population as X-ray samples, and call for targeted X-ray follow-up to pin down scatter and covariance.
