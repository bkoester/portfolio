# A very bright lensed galaxy at z = 1.7

A galaxy cluster magnifies a distant star-forming galaxy into a long, bright arc, letting the team measure
its age, mass and star formation in detail that would otherwise need far larger telescopes.

## Question

What are the stellar population, mass and star formation of the strongly lensed galaxy behind RCS2
032727-132623?

## Data

- Found in RCS2, about 700 deg² of CFHT/MegaCam g, r, z imaging [Sec. I]
- Imaging in nine bands from five telescopes (SOAR, VLT, CFHT, Magellan, APO 3.5 m) [Sec. II.1; Table 1]
- VLT/FORS2 spectrum of the arc, 2 hours total [Sec. II.2]
- Magellan spectroscopy giving 46 cluster members [Sec. II.3]

## Approach

- Measured redshifts of the arc and cluster [Sec. II.2-II.3]
- Built a lens model with LENSTOOL to get magnifications [Sec. IV]
- Fit stellar population models to the arc and its counter-image separately [Sec. V]

## Findings

- Source at z = 1.701 behind a cluster at z = 0.564; the arc spans about 38 arcsec with r = 19.1 [Abstract]
- Cluster velocity dispersion 988 ± 122 km/s [Sec. II.3]
- Magnification 17.2 ± 1.4 for the arc and 2.04 ± 0.16 for the counter-image [Abstract; Sec. IV]
- Best fit: age 80 ± 40 Myr, 0.4 solar metallicity, stellar mass about 10^10 solar masses, star formation
  at most 77 solar masses per year [Sec. VII]
- About 3.5 times brighter than the well-known lensed galaxy cB58 [Sec. VII]

## Why it matters

The authors describe it as a low-mass starburst seen in unusual detail, and the consistent arc and
counter-image results support their photometry, fitting and lens model.
