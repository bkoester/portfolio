# Central galaxies line up with clusters

The brightest galaxy in a cluster tends to point along the cluster's long axis, more so when it dominates its
neighbors, in richer clusters, and nearer to the present day.

## Question

Are brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) aligned with their clusters, and does that depend on BCG dominance,
cluster richness or redshift?

## Data

- SDSS Data Release 6 imaging [Abstract]
- 7031 clusters at 0.08 < z < 0.44 from a matched-filter catalog, covering ≈6500 deg² [Abstract; Table 1]
- 5744 clusters at 0.1 < z < 0.3 from the maxBCG catalog, covering ≈7500 deg² [Abstract; Table 1]

## Approach

- Measured cluster and BCG shapes from second moments of member galaxy and light distributions [Sec. 3.1]
- Called a BCG aligned if within 30° of the cluster axis; compared counts of aligned to non-aligned [Sec. 3.2]
- Split samples by dominance (BCG at least 0.65 mag brighter than the mean of the next two), richness (more
  than 20 red members) and redshift (at 0.26), with K-S tests [Sec. 3.3–3.5]

## Findings

- Aligned-to-non-aligned ratio of 0.753 ± 0.018 for the matched-filter sample, versus 0.5 for random [Table 2]
- Dominant BCGs are more aligned at 4.4σ (4.7σ in the maxBCG sample) [Abstract; Table 4]
- Rich clusters are more aligned than poor ones at 2.3σ [Abstract; Table 6]
- Clusters below z = 0.26 are more aligned than those above at 3.0σ [Abstract; Sec. 3.5]
- Cluster and BCG ellipticities are uncorrelated [Sec. 3.1; Fig. 3]

## Why it matters

Holding in both catalogs, the trends are unlikely to be artifacts; the authors link them to hierarchical
merging along filaments and late secondary infall.
