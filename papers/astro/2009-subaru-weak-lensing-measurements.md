# Are lensing clusters too concentrated?

Combining strong and weak gravitational lensing for four clusters with giant arcs, the paper finds their mass
is more centrally packed than standard cosmology predicts, and more so for clusters with larger Einstein radii.

## Question

Do clusters selected for strong lensing have mass profiles more concentrated than the Lambda-CDM expectation?

## Data

- Four clusters found by the SDSS Giant Arc Survey: Abell 1703, SDSS J1446+3032, SDSS J1531+3414, SDSS J2111-0115 [Sec. 2]
- Redshifts from z = 0.28 to 0.64 [Abstract, Sec. 2]
- Deep Subaru Suprime-Cam g, r, i imaging; r band used for weak lensing [Sec. 2, Table 1]
- Gemini GMOS spectroscopy for three of the clusters [Sec. 2.2]
- About 9,000-10,000 source galaxies per cluster, about 6,000 for SDSS1531 [Sec. 4.2, Table 6]

## Approach

- Model the strong-lensing arcs with an NFW halo plus member galaxies [Sec. 3]
- Measure tangential shear from background galaxy shapes and fit NFW profiles [Sec. 4]
- Combine strong (Einstein radius) and weak constraints into one fit [Sec. 5]
- Compare concentrations with simulation predictions, allowing for lensing selection bias [Sec. 5.2]

## Findings

- NFW profiles describe all four clusters well [Sec. 6]
- Combined concentrations: 6.5 (A1703), 8.3, 7.9 and 14.1 (SDSS2111, poorly constrained) [Table 7]
- Across ten clusters studied with this technique, concentrations exceed Lambda-CDM at 7 sigma, even with a 50% lensing-bias boost [Sec. 5.2]
- The excess grows with Einstein radius; a constant ratio is rejected at 99% confidence [Eq. 19, Fig. 8]
- Abell 1703's inner slope is 0.9 (+0.2/-0.4), consistent with NFW [Appendix A, Fig. 9]

## Why it matters

The authors read the excess as a possible sign of baryon effects, limits of simulations at high mass, or earlier
cluster formation, and call for more arc redshifts and X-ray and SZ data.
