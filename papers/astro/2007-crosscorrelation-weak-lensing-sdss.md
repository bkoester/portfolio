# Weighing SDSS clusters by stacking lensing

Stacking the faint lensing distortions behind about 130,000 SDSS groups and clusters gave their average mass and
density profiles. Mass was tied to a cluster's optical richness to about 13%, across two orders of magnitude in
mass.

## Question

How do cluster mass, density profile, concentration and bias depend on optical richness and luminosity? These
relations are needed before cluster counts can be used for cosmology.

## Data

- About 130,000 maxBCG groups and clusters from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey, with lensing measured from Paper I
  of the series [Abstract; Sec. 1]
- 12 bins in richness (N200) and 16 bins in luminosity (L200) [Sec. 2.1; Tables 1–2]
- Lensing signal measured out to 30 h^-1 Mpc; mean cluster redshift z = 0.25 [Sec. 3.1, 3.3]

## Approach

- Inverted the stacked shear profiles without assuming a shape, giving 3D density and enclosed-mass profiles
  [Sec. 3.1]
- Fitted a halo model that includes clusters whose brightest galaxy is off-center, non-linear shear, the central
  galaxy's own lensing, and neighboring halos [Sec. 4]
- Fitted power laws for mass versus richness and luminosity, and for concentration and bias versus mass [Sec. 5]

## Findings

- Mass at fixed richness or luminosity is measured to about 13%, with statistical and systematic errors included
  [Abstract; Sec. 5.1]
- At N200 = 20, M200 = (8.8 ± 0.4 stat ± 1.1 sys) × 10^13 h^-1 Msun, scaling with richness to the power
  1.28 ± 0.04 [Sec. 5.1, Eq. 26]
- Concentration is about 5 and depends only weakly on mass, with slope −0.12 ± 0.04, consistent with simulations
  [Sec. 5.2, Eq. 28]
- Bias increases with mass, slope 0.38 ± 0.02 [Sec. 5.3, Eq. 29]
- Ignoring miscentering and non-linear shear would bias masses low by roughly a factor of 1.4 [Sec. 5.1]

## Why it matters

Cluster counts constrain σ8 and dark energy only if the mass–observable relation is known. Stacked lensing gives
that relation with little contamination from projection. The authors expect the methods to carry over to DES,
LSST and space-based surveys.
