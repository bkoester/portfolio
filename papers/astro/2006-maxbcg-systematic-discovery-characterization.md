# Finding galaxy clusters in Sloan imaging

Ben's dissertation builds and tests MaxBCG, a method for finding galaxy clusters in large sky-survey images,
and uses it to produce a catalog of 13,823 clusters from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey for use in cosmology.
(Summary written from the Deep Blue abstract; the full text is restricted to U-M users.)

## Question

Can cluster finding be redesigned around modern CCD survey imaging and simulations so that cluster counts
become a usable probe of cosmology?

## Data

- Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) imaging [Abstract]
- Mock galaxy catalogs used to test the method [Abstract]
- Clusters at 0.1 < z < 0.3 with at least 10 bright galaxies [Abstract]

## Approach

- Search for overdensities of bright red galaxies around a candidate brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) [Abstract]
- Measure purity and completeness of the finder on mock catalogs [Abstract]
- Calibrate redshifts and the link between richness and mass using dynamical and weak-lensing estimates [Abstract]
- Introduce a background-corrected richness measure [Abstract]

## Findings

- 13,823 SDSS clusters found, with masses of about 1 x 10^14 solar masses or more [Abstract]
- Photometric redshift errors below 0.015 [Abstract]
- The catalog is close to volume-limited, and its richness tracks dynamical and weak-lensing masses [Abstract]
- The new background-corrected richness carries more mass information than earlier proxies [Abstract]

## Why it matters

The thesis frames this work as laying the groundwork for doing cosmology with optically selected clusters.
