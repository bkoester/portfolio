# How well galaxy counts track cluster mass

Counting a cluster's red galaxies gives a rough mass estimate. Requiring lensing and X-ray data to agree pins
down how rough it is, which cosmology studies need.

## Question

At fixed richness, how widely do maxBCG cluster masses scatter, using only observed quantities?

## Data

- maxBCG catalog: ~14,000 clusters at z 0.1–0.3 from 7500 deg² of SDSS imaging [Sec. II]
- Stacked weak-lensing masses in richness bins [Sec. II]
- Stacked ROSAT All-Sky Survey X-ray luminosities [Sec. II]
- The L_X–mass relation from the 400d survey, covering 397 deg² [Sec. II]

## Approach

- Modeled mass, X-ray luminosity and richness as a joint lognormal distribution [Sec. I.1]
- For trial scatter and correlation values, generated mock clusters and tested whether they reproduce the
  400d L_X–mass relation [Sec. III; Sec. IV]
- Monte Carlo of 3000 draws with 10^5 mock clusters each, marginalizing over nuisance parameters [Sec. IV.2]

## Findings

- Scatter in ln mass at fixed richness is 0.45 (+0.20/−0.18) at 95% confidence, near N200 ≈ 40
  [Abstract; Eq. 15]
- Correlation between L_X and mass scatter at fixed richness is at least 0.85 [Eq. 16]
- Uncertainty in the L_X–mass relation dominates; tighter priors would give 0.42 (+0.07/−0.09) [Sec. V; Fig. 3]
- The implied mass function at z = 0.23 matches WMAP5 for 0.724 ≤ σ8 ≤ 0.868 [Sec. VII; Fig. 5]

## Why it matters

Scatter biases cosmological constraints from cluster counts; this measures it without assuming a cosmology.
