# How far away are giant lensed arcs?

Measuring distances to a complete set of giant gravitational arcs showed the lensed galaxies sit farther away
than older predictions assumed, which may help explain why more arcs are seen than theory expected.

## Question

What is the redshift distribution of the background galaxies that appear as giant arcs, measured from a
uniformly selected, spectroscopically complete sample?

## Data

- 28 giant arcs from the Sloan Giant Arcs Survey: 24 with precise Gemini/GMOS-North redshifts and 4 with
  redshift-desert limits [Abstract; Sec. II]
- 19 additional lensed sources found in deeper Gemini pre-imaging, a less uniform secondary sample [Abstract; Sec. II.3]
- Lensing clusters with median redshift 0.435 [Sec. II.1]

## Approach

- Selected arcs by eye in SDSS imaging and followed them up spectroscopically [Sec. II.2]
- Built a model combining simulated lensing cross-sections with COSMOS galaxy counts by redshift [Sec. III.1]
- Compared data and model with Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests under several assumptions [Sec. III.2; Table 2]

## Findings

- Median arc redshift of 1.821 for the primary sample [Abstract; Sec. IV]
- 64% (18 of 28) of arcs lie at z ≳ 1.45 [Sec. IV]
- Best model match gives a KS probability of 0.738 [Sec. III.2; Table 2]
- Adding the secondary sources, no model tested agrees well with the data [Sec. III.2; Table 2]

## Why it matters

The authors suggest the long-standing excess of giant arcs over predictions may partly come from earlier work
placing all source galaxies at z = 1.
