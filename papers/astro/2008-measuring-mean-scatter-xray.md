# How X-ray bright are optically found clusters?

Stacking X-ray survey data on thousands of optically found clusters measured how X-ray brightness rises with
cluster richness, and how widely it varies. The spread explains why optical clusters had seemed X-ray faint.

## Question

What is the mean X-ray luminosity of maxBCG clusters as a function of optical richness, and how much does it
scatter from cluster to cluster?

## Data

- maxBCG catalog: 13,823 clusters over about 7,500 square degrees, plus 3,532 added clusters with N200 = 9
  [Sec. II.2]
- More than 17,000 clusters at 0.1 < z < 0.3 in all [Abstract]
- ROSAT All-Sky Survey soft X-ray data (0.1-2.4 keV), typical exposure about 400 s in the region [Sec. II.3]
- SDSS DR5 imaging [Sec. II.1]

## Approach

- Stacked X-ray photons around each cluster's brightest galaxy in nine richness bins [Sec. III; Table 1]
- Fit radial profiles and spectra of the stacks to get mean luminosities [Sec. III.3-III.4]
- Measured scatter from 955 rich clusters (N200 >= 30) with individual fluxes [Sec. IV.1]
- Compared with clusters from the X-ray selected NORAS and 400d catalogs [Sec. IV.2]

## Findings

- Mean X-ray luminosity measured to about 10%, systematics included, in nine richness bins [Abstract]
- About 800 of the richest clusters are crudely detected one by one [Abstract]
- Log-normal scatter at fixed richness of 0.86 ± 0.03 [Sec. IV.1]
- Bias-corrected median relation with normalization 3.57 ± 0.08 and slope 1.82 ± 0.05 [Abstract]
- Nearly all 89 matched NORAS clusters sit above the median maxBCG relation, and the 53 deeper 400d matches are
  still biased high [Sec. IV.2, Fig. 8]

## Why it matters

The authors show that X-ray selection picks the brightest clusters at a given richness, which accounts for the
apparent X-ray faintness of optically selected catalogs.
