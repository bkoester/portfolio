# Cosmology from counting maxBCG clusters

Counting maxBCG clusters and weighing them with weak lensing pins down how clumpy the universe is, with
precision matching X-ray cluster studies; it was the first optical catalog to reach that level.

## Question

What do the abundance and lensing masses of SDSS maxBCG clusters say about sigma_8 and Omega_m, together with the
richness-mass relation?

## Data

- maxBCG clusters from 7,398 square degrees of SDSS imaging, photometric redshift 0.1 to 0.3 [Sec. 2]
- Nine richness bins over N200 from 11 to 120, plus five richer clusters treated one by one [Table 1]
- Stacked weak-lensing masses in five richness bins [Table 2]
- WMAP5, supernova and BAO data for combined fits [Sec. 4, Table 6]

## Approach

- Bayesian fit of cosmology and the richness-mass relation at the same time, in flat Lambda-CDM [Sec. 3]
- Model counts from the halo mass function, with photometric redshift scatter and purity/completeness [Sec. 3.2-3.3]
- Priors on the lensing mass bias and on mass scatter at fixed richness [Sec. 3.4, Table 3]
- Tests of each systematic by loosening priors and model forms [Sec. 5]

## Findings

- sigma_8 (Omega_m/0.25)^0.41 = 0.832 ± 0.033 after marginalizing over systematics [Eq. 18]
- With WMAP5: sigma_8 = 0.807 ± 0.020 and Omega_m = 0.265 ± 0.016, about twice as tight as WMAP5 alone [Eq. 19, Abstract]
- Richness-mass slope 0.757 ± 0.066 and scatter 0.357 ± 0.073 [Sec. 4.2]
- Agrees with three X-ray cluster abundance studies [Table 5, Fig. 13]
- Adding maxBCG to WMAP5 + supernovae tightens w to -0.978 ± 0.053 [Table 6]

## Why it matters

The authors show optically selected clusters can give tight, robust cosmology, with the lensing mass scale and
richness-mass scatter as the main error sources to improve.
