# Turning administrative records into research data

How the University of Michigan built a shared, flat, documented research dataset from its administrative records,
so researchers stopped re-cleaning the same data for every project, and a six-point framework for other
institutions to do the same.

## Question

How can an institution make its administrative student data usable for learning-analytics research, and keep it
useful as the research community grows?

## Data

University of Michigan administrative records: academic careers, demographics, test scores, course grades and
transfer credit.

## Approach

- A cross-campus team of faculty, staff, students and IT formed in early 2015; design took about six months, and
  the dataset was released in September 2016 [Sec. 3.2, 3.5]
- Designed around the researcher: as few as five flat tables, values recoded to simple 0/1 flags, common
  calculations such as prior-term GPA built in [Sec. 3.3, 3.4]
- Built to answer about 80% of researchers' common questions, and extensible for the rest [Sec. 3.3]
- Released as snapshots three times a year, each preserving the full known history, a practice borrowed from the
  Sloan Digital Sky Survey's data releases [Sec. 3.5.1]
- Two delivery routes, a database and flat files, plus a public data dictionary, a mock dataset and a user forum
  [Sec. 3.6]

## Findings

- More than 50 investigators had requested and received access by the time of writing [Sec. 4, 6]
- Access from request to data took about a week, through a documented approval process [Sec. 3.6.3]
- The dataset enabled analyses such as course-by-major grade maps, co-enrollment networks, course-sequence
  explorers and matched-peer career plots [Sec. 4]
- Six recommendations for other institutions, including finding the "80% rule" early, getting buy-in from data
  stewards, and designing toward national data standards [Sec. 5]
- Its development model was reused for a later institutional enrollment dataset [Sec. 6]

## Why it matters

A repeatable model for making administrative data research-ready, adaptable to any institution with digitized
records and willing researchers.
