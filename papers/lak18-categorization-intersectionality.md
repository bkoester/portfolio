# Social identity in learning analytics

Learning analytics usually relies on the simplified identity categories institutions record. This extended
abstract argues that using them without scrutiny hides the complexity of who students are, and draws on
intersectionality research to do better.

## Question

How should learning analytics handle social identity, and how can intersectionality research shape that work?

## Data

A set of large introductory courses; no further detail in the abstract.

## Approach

Draws on feminist intersectionality scholarship to guide analysis of how social identity relates to performance
in large introductory courses.

## Findings

From the abstract only. The central argument: reductive identity categories, used uncritically, obscure students'
individual experience.

## Why it matters

The authors frame it as a precondition for learning analytics that serves all students.
